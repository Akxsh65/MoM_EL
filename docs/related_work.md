## Findings
- Re-check 2026-11-28: Gulati et al. 2016 (row 2) report 87% on the 480-recording, 40-raga Carnatic set and 98% on a 300-recording, 30-raga Hindustani set with a k-NN on the TDMS (abstract/postprint page). Row 11 was mis-described earlier ("Hindustani alap sections"): it is Dhrupad alap structural segmentation. Row 12's authors are Dutta, Sekhar PV and Murthy (Carnatic, not generic).
- Closest to us in *time*: Chordia & Rae 2008 (read in full) and Rāgawise (read in full). Both show how each raga's likelihood evolves while listening (online, Hindustani). Neither evaluates where a raga *changes*: Chordia & Rae report only informal convergence times for single-raga performances (about 15 s to a correct label, 30-40 s to stable) and one anecdote of a quick shift when the performer introduced another raag's phrase; Rāgawise has no quantitative evaluation. Sarala & Murthy (row 6) and Padi & Murthy (row 7) segment concerts into items; Sarala & Murthy note that detecting a raga change is what separates inter- from intra-item applause, but assume one raga per item (rows 6 and 7 not read in full).
- Closest to us in *features*: TDMS (row 2) and sequential pitch distributions (row 15); both classify whole recordings.
- Extra searches on 2026-11-28 ("raga change detection", "raga boundary", "ragamalika segmentation", "raga transition within a recording") found no paper that performs windowed rāga recognition with temporal decoding and evaluates rāga-change boundaries. This is an absence in a handful of web searches, not proof: Scholar / ISMIR-archive full-text searches and a check of citing papers of rows 6, 7 and 15 are still advisable.
